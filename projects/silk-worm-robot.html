Silkworm robot

The paper of this project is currently under review at IEEE, targeting a Q1 publication. Our work presents a novel robotic design inspired by silkworms, aimed at enhancing capabilities for monitoring, surveillance, and inspection on various metal surfaces. Below is three samples of our experimnets and the abstract of our paper, outlining the key findings and contributions:

" In this paper, we present a novel, modular, compliant, and bio-inspired robotic system designed for ferromagnetic surface (i.e., iron) inspection. Inspired by the body morphology of silkworms, the design consists of three modules equipped with a passive magnetic adhesion system, connected via a spring-joint mechanism. This modular and compliant design ensures a high level of maneuverability. Additionally, the magnetic adhesion system provides significant stability and robustness during complex maneuvers on ferromagnetic surfaces. The compliant body also enhances the robot’s energy efficiency during long-term locomotion. The maneuverability, stability, robustness, and energy efficiency of the design are assessed through both analytical and experimental studies. In our analytical studies, we compute the general form of surface stability, the lower bound for magnetic adhesion force required for complex maneuvers and robustness in falling conditions, and the minimum radius for surface turning maneuvers. We also demonstrate that the proposed design can generate an energy-efficient cyclic crawling pattern compared to non-cyclic (simple) locomotion. Experimental studies confirm the robot’s ability to stably transition across different types of internal and external corners and cylindrical structures. To test the robot’s robustness in falling scenarios, we check if a single module can attach to the ferromagnetic surface when other modules are falling. For locomotion efficiency, we exploit the bio-inspired locomotion of our robot, which, compared to simple locomotion, results in an average 6.7% lower cost of transportation and a 10.2% higher forward velocity. The proposed experimental results, along with analytical studies, confirm the efficacy of the design as an ideal solution for the remote inspection of complex structures with ferromagnetic surfaces."
